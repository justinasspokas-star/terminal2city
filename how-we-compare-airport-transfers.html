Editorial methodology

How Terminal2City compares airport transfers

We do not call one option “best” for everyone. We compare the whole airport-to-destination journey and explain which option fits a traveller’s actual circumstances.

Methodology updated · 26 September 2026
1. Final destination

We look beyond the city name. Liverpool Street, Victoria and Paddington are all “London”, but they can produce different practical routes from the same airport.

2. Arrival terminal

Terminal access changes the real journey. Heathrow Terminal 4 can add an airport connection before some coach options, while Gatwick North Terminal adds the free shuttle before using the South Terminal rail station.

3. Journey effort

We make friction visible using four practical signals: changes, walking, luggage effort and whether the option is door to door. “Low”, “Medium” and “Higher” describe the trip structure, not transport quality.

4. Whole journey + arrival time

Headline journey time is only one part of the trip. We include terminal access, interchanges and the final leg, and flag late-night cases when service patterns can change.

5. Group + luggage

We compare the practicality of multiple public-transport tickets against a door-to-door vehicle, without pretending that one is always cheaper.

6. Live verification

Timetables, fares and disruptions change. Each guide links to official operator information so the traveller can verify the live situation before booking.

What “best fit” means

A recommendation is based on trade-offs, not a hidden ranking

Our interactive route check uses simple, explainable rules and shows the journey effort behind the suggestion. For example, a direct train may be a strong fit for one traveller going to a station-centre destination, while a group with four suitcases heading to an outlying address may reasonably prefer a private transfer.

We show the reason for the result and at least one alternative. The traveller makes the final choice.

What we do not do

We do not present sample prices as live fares, hide changes in the journey, or claim that an option is universally fastest or cheapest when that depends on date, traffic, ticket type or destination.

Source policy

Operator information comes first

For route facts such as service patterns, terminal locations and published journey guidance, we prefer airport and transport-operator sources. Each route page carries a “checked” date and direct links to the relevant live source.

Commercial relationships

If Terminal2City later earns a referral fee or commission from a booking link, that relationship should be disclosed. Commercial availability does not change the editorial explanation of directness, changes, luggage friction or destination fit.
