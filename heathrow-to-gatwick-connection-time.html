Heathrow to Gatwick Airport connection time: is 3, 4, 5 or 6 hours enough?

Landing at Heathrow and flying out of Gatwick? The inter-airport coach may take around 70 minutes, but that is only one part of a self-transfer. Use this guide to work out immigration, luggage, waiting time, the journey, terminal access and how early your airline expects you at Gatwick.

Quick answer

The 70-minute coach journey is not your minimum connection time

Heathrow lists a 70-minute direct coach journey on National Express or FlixBus and approximately 75 minutes on The Airline, but you must first leave Heathrow and then complete departure formalities at Gatwick. Gatwick recommends arriving at least two hours before your flight, subject to your airline's own requirements. For a self-transfer, three or four hours between flights may be especially tight. Five or six hours give you more room, but the answer depends on your exact itinerary. There is no universal, guaranteed Heathrow–Gatwick minimum connection time.

Direct coach: simplest on paper, but departures matter

The Heathrow–Gatwick direct coach can be convenient because you avoid changing trains in central London. You still need to reach its Heathrow stop and identify which Gatwick terminal it serves. For Heathrow Terminals 2 and 3, National Express uses Heathrow Central Bus Station; other terminals have their own stops. If your arriving flight is late, the next available departure can change your plan. Don't assume the coach runs exactly when you leave baggage reclaim.

Check National Express's date-specific timetable, FlixBus or The Airline. Verify the correct direction (Heathrow → Gatwick), stops, service frequency and booking flexibility.

Train: usually change at Farringdon

The practical rail option Heathrow lists is Elizabeth line to Farringdon, followed by Thameslink to Gatwick Airport. It can make sense when road conditions are poor, but a 35-minute rail leg followed by a 40-minute leg is not a 75-minute airport transfer: you still have to wait, change platforms and get into Gatwick departures. Check railway engineering works, the correct Heathrow station and the onward Thameslink service. The Gatwick rail station is in South Terminal; if you depart from North Terminal, take the free inter-terminal shuttle and allow time to reach security.

Taxi: value for a group, but not automatically faster

A pre-booked car can remove coach waiting and platform changes and may suit families with bags, but it does not eliminate road traffic or late flight issues. Ask for the full vehicle fare, an airport pickup location, whether the driver tracks the flight and the waiting-time policy. Compare the full door-to-door journey, not just the car's advertised drive time.

Self-transfer with separate flight tickets: what changes?

Changing airports means leaving Heathrow's airside transit system. You need to enter the UK where applicable, exit through arrivals and make your way independently to Gatwick. If you have checked baggage, plan to reclaim it at Heathrow and hand it over again at Gatwick unless your airline has explicitly confirmed another arrangement. Even without checked baggage, you must reach Gatwick, pass security and meet the airline's boarding requirements.

Heathrow explains that travellers who have booked flights separately are self-connecting passengers, not passengers following the usual airside purple “Flight Connections” signs. Confirm that your passport, visa or immigration status allows you to enter the UK to make the transfer. This is particularly important for international arrivals; requirements vary by nationality, residence and travel purpose.

At Gatwick, check the terminal on your boarding pass and the airline's bag-drop and check-in closing times. Gatwick generally recommends reaching the airport at least two hours before departure, but its check-in areas and individual airline procedures differ. A second flight's departure time is later than the deadline to complete check-in, and the boarding gate can close before the departure time.

Two tickets can mean two separate risks

If your first flight arrives late and you miss a separately booked Gatwick departure, the second airline may not be obliged to rebook you without charge. A protected connecting itinerary or a travel insurance policy can have different terms. Never assume a missed connection is automatically covered.

How to decide whether your Heathrow–Gatwick connection is too short

  1. Check UK entry requirements before you travel. An airport-to-airport transfer is not the same as staying airside on one booking.
  2. Use the actual arriving Heathrow terminal. Terminal 5 pickup can differ from a coach leaving Heathrow Central Bus Station.
  3. Include your bags and immigration. Arriving with carry-on only may save time, but passport queues are still uncertain.
  4. Search dated coach or rail departures. Don't plan around a quoted 70-minute trip if the next coach won't leave for 45 minutes.
  5. Check Gatwick North or South Terminal. The rail station is at South; the terminal shuttle adds another step for North departures.
  6. Work backwards from the airline's requirements. The latest acceptable bag-drop time and required arrival allowance matter more than the second flight's gate departure alone.
  7. Budget for disruption. Even a technically possible connection may be a poor choice if missing the second flight is expensive.

Before booking the second flight: quick checklist

  • Is it one protected itinerary or two separately purchased tickets?
  • Do you have the documents and permission required to enter the UK?
  • Which terminal will you arrive at in Heathrow and depart from at Gatwick?
  • Will you collect checked baggage at Heathrow and recheck it at Gatwick?
  • Have you checked your airline's check-in, bag drop, security and boarding deadlines?
  • Does your exact coach/train run on your travel date and call at the correct stop?
  • How will you handle a 60–90 minute first-flight delay or heavy road traffic?

Frequently asked questions about Heathrow to Gatwick connections

Is 3 hours enough to transfer from Heathrow to Gatwick?

Three hours is generally a very high-risk self-transfer window: immigration, baggage reclaim, waiting for transport, the inter-airport journey, and Gatwick check-in/security must all fit. This is not an airport-approved minimum connection time; calculate your own steps and airline cutoff.

Is 4 hours enough between Heathrow and Gatwick?

Four hours may be too tight, especially with checked baggage or separate tickets. A direct coach's published 70-minute journey excludes time leaving Heathrow, waiting for transport and processing at Gatwick. Use the calculator and check the real timetable.

Is 5 or 6 hours enough for Heathrow to Gatwick?

A five- or six-hour gap can be more practical than three or four hours, but there is no universal guarantee. Your inbound delays, passport clearance, baggage, coach departure and airline check-in deadlines determine whether it works.

Is there a direct train from Heathrow to Gatwick?

No. Heathrow's official guide says there is no direct rail service between the two airports. Common rail routes require changing in central London, such as the Elizabeth line to Farringdon followed by Thameslink to Gatwick.

How long does the Heathrow to Gatwick coach take?

Heathrow quotes about 70 minutes on direct National Express or FlixBus coach services and about 75 minutes on The Airline. These are approximate transport times, not total flight-to-flight connection times.

Do I have to collect my baggage between Heathrow and Gatwick?

If you are changing airports, plan on passing through arrivals and collecting checked bags at Heathrow, then checking in again at Gatwick unless your airline confirms an alternative arrangement. Airport changes are not an airside connection.

What if I land at Heathrow Terminal 5 and fly from Gatwick North Terminal?

Account for reaching the correct Heathrow Terminal 5 pickup point and reaching Gatwick North Terminal after the coach or car journey. Check the coach stops on your specific ticket. Gatwick's train station is at South Terminal, connected to North by a free shuttle.

What happens if the Heathrow flight is delayed and I miss my Gatwick flight?

If the flights were booked separately, rebooking protection is not automatic. Contact the departing airline immediately, review your tickets, travel insurance and any package or connecting-itinerary terms, and keep delay and transport receipts.
